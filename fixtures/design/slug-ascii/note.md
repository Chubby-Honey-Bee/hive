A correct design decides: byte-wise scanning (non-ASCII is never kept, so no rune decoding is needed); a run of separators collapses to one hyphen and the ends are trimmed; the 32-byte cut happens after trimming and prefers the last hyphen at or before byte 32, falling back to a hard cut; `untitled` for an empty result; and that the cut rule keeps the function idempotent (a cut slug has no trailing hyphen). The twin, slug-unicode, keeps non-ASCII letters and has no cut, so a design that ignores the statement fails one of the two.
